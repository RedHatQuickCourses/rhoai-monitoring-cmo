= Alerting for AI Model Serving
:navtitle: Alerting

== Introduction

You now have three operational dashboards in Grafana.
GPU health, inference latency, queue depth, KV cache -- it is all there, updating in real time.
But here is the problem: dashboards only work when someone is watching them.

At 2 AM on a Saturday, nobody is watching.
Your model server is saturated, requests are queuing, users are getting 15-second response times, and your dashboard panels are glowing red -- but nobody is looking.

This is the gap alerts fill.
Dashboards are for humans who are already watching.
Alerts are for humans who are doing something else.
An alert reaches out and says: "Something needs your attention."
Then you open the dashboard to figure out what.

This chapter teaches you what makes a good alert, how to structure alert rules as Kubernetes resources, how to choose thresholds that are meaningful without being noisy, and how to observe the full alert lifecycle under load.

== Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

. Explain what makes a useful alert and apply three evaluation questions to any proposed alert rule
. Describe the PrometheusRule YAML structure and the role of the `expr`, `for`, `labels`, and `annotations` fields
. Explain why OpenShift namespace enforcement requires GPU and vLLM alert rules to live in separate namespaces
. Deploy PrometheusRule resources that define infrastructure and application alerts for AI model serving
. Observe the full alert lifecycle -- Inactive, Pending, Firing, Resolved -- under a controlled load test
